# MarketSprint — Market & Competitor Research

## Research question

Is there room for a student-focused product that combines stock-market education with structured paper-trading practice and measurable progress?

## Market observation

The Indian retail-investor ecosystem already has strong products for education, charting, paper trading and market information. The opportunity is therefore not to build another generic stock app, but to solve the beginner learning journey more deliberately.

## Competitor snapshot

| Product | Strength | Gap / opportunity for MarketSprint |
|---|---|---|
| TradingView | Excellent charting and paper trading | More powerful than beginner-focused; not primarily a guided student curriculum |
| Zerodha Varsity | Free, structured financial education | Education-first; less centered on a gamified learn-to-practice loop |
| StockGro | Social learning, challenges and market simulation | MarketSprint can focus more tightly on structured skill development and risk discipline |
| Moneycontrol | Broad market information and financial content | Information-rich rather than a dedicated practice curriculum |

## Customer needs to test

1. Beginners want a simple place to start.
2. Students want practical experience without losing real money.
3. Learners need feedback after making a simulated decision.
4. Short challenges may be easier to complete than long courses.
5. Students may enjoy cohort or campus competition if it reinforces learning.
6. Risk management should be treated as a skill, not an afterthought.

## Social-information insight

SEBI's 2025 investor survey indicates that investors commonly use interpersonal networks, financial influencers and online investment communities when obtaining investment information. This reinforces the need for financial-learning products that help users evaluate information and build independent decision-making skills rather than simply follow tips.

## Competitive positioning

MarketSprint should own the phrase/concept:

> **Practice the market before you risk your money.**

The product should feel like a training ground rather than a brokerage account.

## Validation hypotheses

### H1 — Problem
At least 60% of surveyed students interested in markets report that learning feels fragmented or that they lack a structured way to practice.

### H2 — Solution
At least 50% say they would try a free 7-day learn-and-paper-trade challenge.

### H3 — Retention
At least 30% of challenge participants complete the full 7 days.

### H4 — Differentiation
Users describe the combination of lessons + simulation + review as more useful than consuming market content alone.

### H5 — Distribution
Finance clubs, student communities and campus competitions can become acquisition channels.

## Survey approach

Start with a small validation survey and use it to improve the MVP rather than to prove the idea. Aim for at least 30 responses for the first directional read and 100+ responses for a stronger student-market signal.

## Decision rule

Proceed to a functional MVP if users demonstrate both **stated demand** and **behavioral demand**. Behavioral demand means signing up, completing lessons, making simulated trades and returning to review performance.
